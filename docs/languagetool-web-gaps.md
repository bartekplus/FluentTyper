# Gaps against the LanguageTool website (2026-10-30)

## Why the earlier parity work missed these errors

- The earlier parity pass compared FluentTyper with the example sentences of LanguageTool's open-source rule files (`grammar.xml`, `style.xml`).
- The LanguageTool website and the public API with `abtest=qb` also run a **neural correction model**. Its findings have rule ids that start with `QB_NEW_EN_`. This model is not in the open-source rule files.
- The example sentence of the report ("…try to stop him doing anything") gets **no** finding from the open-source rules (the free API, default and picky levels). Both website findings come from the neural model:
  - `QB_NEW_EN_OTHER_ERROR_IDS_7`: "him" → "him from"
  - `QB_NEW_EN_OTHER`: "anything" → "anything?"
- On the written corpus below, 113 of the 121 errors that only LanguageTool found came from the neural model.

## Method

- Corpus 1: 351 sentence pairs (error and correction) in 29 error families, written for this evaluation, plus 9 clean control sentences.
- Corpus 2: JFLEG learner sentences (1,304 with errors, 1,501 references), detokenized.
- Clean corpus for false positives: 11,259 sentences that LanguageTool marks as correct in its rule files, plus the corrected sentences of corpus 1 and `tests/fixtures/native-review-corpus/reference.txt`. Each change was checked against the findings of master on this corpus; every new finding was read.
- FluentTyper: the default Review rules and the offline spelling pass (en_US).
- LanguageTool: the website configuration (`level=picky`, `abtest=qb`, neural model on).
- The corpora and the harness stay outside the repository. No LanguageTool text is in the repository; the tests use our own sentences.

- On corpus 1, default Review now finds as many errors as the LanguageTool website (272 against 273) and has no false positives (LanguageTool: 30).
- The example of the report now gets both website findings: "him" → "him from" and "anything" → "anything?".

- JFLEG stays far behind: most of its remaining LanguageTool findings are spelling suggestions for heavy learner misspellings and whole-sentence rewrites from the neural model. Rules cannot reach those without the opt-in Local AI.

## Not done, and why

- Existing project decisions win over the website: adverb placement ("They walk often to school") stays in the optional style check, "The advices from abroad" stays (a trade sense), "Please login to continue" and "look forward to" + a noun stay as the existing rules decide, and "Her and her sister moved away" stays unflagged.
- Speed: `bun run bench:review` gives en_US a median of 6.21 ms on this branch against 5.83 ms on master on the same, loaded machine (minimum 4.83 against 4.82 ms). The 5 ms limit needs a quiet machine to judge.

- Style advice that LanguageTool flags by default ("try and", "the reason is because", "very unique", "return back", "do the needful"): these are register choices, not errors. They stay in the optional style checks.
- Dialect and informal usage ("I ain't got no", "There's lots of", "stop him doing" in British English): accepted in their register. "stop him doing" is flagged only because Review checks US English.
- Ambiguous constructions where a rule cannot tell the meaning: "kept him freezing", "I'm boring in this class", "My friend she lives", "interested to learn" (correct English), "Neither of the options seem" (accepted).
- Questions on one line with no mark and no text before them ("Would it be possible to move the call to Thursday"): they look like headings.
